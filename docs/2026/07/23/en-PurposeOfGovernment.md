# Purpose of the government

Society is the grand total of all people and their interactions, both among themselves as well as with their environment. The Dutch society is the grand total of everyone with Dutch citizenship and their interactions.

The way we treat each other and our environment determines the appearance of our society. We trade (work for money, money for goods and services that we need or make us happy). We agree on rules to ensure that the freedom of one individual does not harm the freedom of others. We maintain institutions that punish the violation of these rules, institutions that teach young people how the world works as well as why and how we collectively uphold and revise agreements and laws.

On the other hand we must not forget that the impact of these institutions on society is limited. Exercising these functions is the work of ordinary people. Mistakes are made, means are limited, some of the people who work in institutions have less than idealistic motives. Permits are granted unjustly and objection procedures can be misused. Criminals are not always caught. Children learn that bullying pays off and compensate low self-esteem with a bloated social profile.

What is in all this the purpose of the government.

Not very long ago, a few centuries or so, the government was an organization that, on behalf of a ruling class (land-owners and clergy at first, joined by rich groups like bankers and industrialists later), ensured that the wealth of the ruling class was maximized. That meant that most other people needed to be sufficiently fed, clothed and housed for them to be productive, but no more.

With the rise of cities and industrialization that was no longer sufficient. There was an increasing demand for schooled workers. These schooled workers did not accept the low standards of living that the production workers were accustomed to. So, mainly in the cities, they organized institutions that made higher standards of living possible for larger groups of people. Because large groups of people became less powerless, these institutions could merge with the existing government and transform it to a democratic foundation for society.

A long story to clarify that the democratic constitutional state is not simply given, but that it was wrought with blood, sweat and tears. We are desperately going to regret it if it were to collapse.

And it is collapsing!

Slowly but surely institutions succumb to the burdens that we place on them (often in the form of micromanagement). Slowly but surely the trust in institutions declines. The efforts of minorities (and truly everybody belongs to some minority) to further their specific interest grows and the efforts of moderate people to support society as a whole are very low.

What has changed?

After World War Two, the motivation of virtually everybody to rebuild society was very high. A number of generations worked very very hard for this. After that it seemed that the only thing left to do was management. Where possible, 'key performance indicators' (KPI's) were made measurable and the government maximizes the gross national product beyond our wildest dreams. Maybe with the benefit of hindsight not so strange, but the economic progress did not really translate to a nicer life for average people. Yes, the amount of stuff and the quality of housing increases, but social well-being declines and the available housing doesn't keep up with the growth of the population. The increased gross national product does not end up in the treasury of the government, neither is it reflected in higher salaries. How is that possible?

Well, everybody is glued to their phones (remind me, why has social well-being declined the last two decades), and Big Tech companies are insanely wealthy (as in: richer than some governments).

What are we going to do about it?

## Insights

I don't think there is a simple solution. However, I believe the following insights are valuable when navigating this crisis.

### Cynefin

According to the [Cynefin](https://thecynefin.co/about-us/about-cynefin-framework/) model, the playing field that people find themselves in (be it a family, a sports club or the government) can be categorized as Clear (or Simple), Complicated, Complex or Chaotic. Every category needs a different style of management. In recent decades we leave more and more to the market of supply and demand. Not just complicated problems, but increasingly also complex problems. The result is that smart (but morally challenged) business people pocket the compensation for solving these problems; in exchange, however, their business delivers only value on paper. They don't actually contribute substantially to society. (See also [Technocarcinization](https://pluralistic.net/2026/07/01/ontogeny/#recapitulates-phylogeny) by Cory Doctorow.) Bluntly stated: they steal from the tax payer.

### Culture

Alberto Brandolini once posited the definition: "culture is the cumulative effect of a winning behavior in a given context" ([photo](2026/07/23/brandolini.jpg)). In many cases, it is not effective to punish unwanted behavior to change a destructive culture. Find the cause. Offer a better way to obtain the same personal value without the negative collective consequences.

### Addiction

An addiction is a solution that compounds the problem (I think I read this in \[[Geurtz1999](#!@bib@bibliography.json@Geurtz1999)], but I'm afraid someone borrowed it from me, because I can't find the book). This insight is heavily related to culture. Offer a long-term solution for a real problem and the need to care for addicted people diminishes. This is just as true for addiction to social media as for addiction to hard-drugs.

### Conway's Law

Melvin Conway stated: "organizations which design systems \[...] are constrained to produce designs which are copies of the communication structures of these organizations" (see [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway%27s_law)). So if you sign a single contract with a large supplier that promises to solve all your problems, you will get a product that is designed according to an organizational chart that has no relation whatsoever to the value that the supplier is supposed to add to your organization.

The solution is interoperability. Define standards for common products that guarantee that products from different suppliers are trivially interchangeable. In a free market, suppliers that deliver working products at low cost will automatically rise to the surface.

Monstrous European tenders induce wasteful spending. Take care: opposite economies of scale, there are also drawbacks of scale. The largest supplier does probably not offer the best buy.

### In theory, there is no difference between theory and practice

The way we think about governance and regulation is quite naive. When I look around me, I see a lot of bodies that govern (not only governmental bodies, but also boards of directors of companies and foundation boards) write memo's that state values, principles, guard rails, rules and laws that are either stating the obvious or completely disconnected from the context in which they are supposed to be applied. I got a lot of insights from the book [The Fifth Discipline](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Fifth_Discipline) \[[Senge2010](#!@bib@bibliography.json@Senge2010)] about how organizations work in practice (as opposed to the so called reality on paper). Especially the observation that almost everything is influenced by positive and/or negative feedback and the impact of the delay between cause and effect thoroughly changed my understanding of group behavior.

The gap between theory and practice is highly related to this definition of insanity: repeating the same actions over and over and expecting different results. Conviction: in theory this should work; evaluation: it did not, however (again).

The difference between theory and practice also has a huge impact on the approach of (self-)destructive expressions of culture. It is futile to try and proscribe (or suppress) culture. We have to search for feasible aspects in the context of collective behavior that can be the cause of cultural change. Analysis and fix only works as a strategy for Complicated problems. Complex problems like culture-change can only be addressed by combining experimentation with reinforcement of desired behavior.

## What should the government do?

The market of supply and demand is not going to save democracy. In any case, the market is no longer free, because monopolies can do almost whatever they want in the current global economy.

Friendly countries are not going to save our democracy. Listen, for example, to [Mark Carney in Davos](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=sFBkNX1-cbg).

Interest groups (environmental, social, labor unions, etc.) are not going to save democracy, because they are in conflict with each other.

Government, get your act together: set limits!

For a healthy democracy, we need:

* A truly *free* market of supply and demand
    * Enforce interoperability for products that have become commodities
    * Prevent and combat black holes of wealth
    * Strictly regulate monopolies, including the dealer-like behavior to inseparably intertwine access to phone, messaging and calendar with addictive apps by combining a single mobile device per user with practically mandatory subscriptions — like insulin en heroin in a single syringe
    * Strictly regulate monopolies; prevent them from hanging a sword of Damocles over our society by means of a kill-switch 
* Solidarity: money doesn't buy happiness; stimulate citizens to spend effort for the public good
* A stable distribution of income: what are the dynamics that skew income inequality more and more and stabilize it — the goal should not be total equality of income, but prevent that we slide back to feudalism!
